Optimal strategy for trading on telegram group calls

Scope of analysis

One unnamed Telegram group, October 16–21, 2026. The primary refit uses 766 human-authored calls among 1506 saved eligible calls. Automated posts and scanner replies are excluded from its entry signals, caller history, training and account replays. Prices come from saved one-second market data. An inherited fixed-rule control also restricts entries and reference histories to human calls, but uses settings selected in earlier research. All results are retrospective simulations, and no live return is measured.

The search compares 160 distinct human-only entry masks, including market-cap bands below $6k and $10k and above $1m, chain, quote freshness, token age and earlier caller outcomes. It tests 6 entry delays from one to sixty seconds and 64 exit rules, including 5–180-second holds, fixed stops, 1.1×–3× targets and trailing exits. Position sizes of 1%, 2% and 5% are evaluated on shortlisted policies. The full screen covers 61,440 combinations.

Interpretation: Selection and evaluation reuse dates already examined in prior research. The separately frozen later windows have produced no qualifying trades. The figures below do not establish an independently validated profitable strategy.

Inherited fixed-rule sensitivity

An earlier fixed 3× target with a five-minute limit was replayed using only human entry calls and earlier human reference calls. Its best of four post hoc reference and size variants returned at least +1.68% across 25 saved execution scenarios and two human-entry populations, with at least 7 trades. Its worst return after removing the two best tokens was +0.36%; after removing the best entry day, +1.02%. The entry settings came from an earlier broader historical search, so this control is not a fresh human-only fit or independent validation. All four variants and scenario results.

On the inherited five-minute fixed rule, the existing group sizing returns at least +1.48% across the saved execution scenarios at a 10% haircut. The best of 144 additional recent-score variants returns at least +2.08%, but its drawdown bound reaches 1.12% against 0.94% for the existing factor. After removing the two best tokens, the respective worst returns are +0.15% and +0.27%. The new variant stakes more, and its profit per dollar staked exceeds the existing factor in only 49 of 100 scenarios.

Gradually raising size as the group does worse has weak support here. The best reversal variant on the guarded daily procedure returns -7.08% at a 10% sale haircut versus -7.44% for flat full size; both lose. On the inherited fixed rule, the best reversal variant returns at least +1.29% versus +1.48% for its existing size factor, with drawdown bounds of 1.60% and 0.94% respectively. Its median stake is 7.88 versus 8.11 for flat full size, and it beats that flat rule's account return in only 22 of 100 execution cases. The gentler slopes and zero-haircut results are in the linked detail.

Among later first human calls with enough history, the next-token mean was -14.08% in the lowest recent-score quartile and -29.24% in the highest. A better recent group score did not predict better next-token returns in this sample. The sizing grid was searched on previously examined dates.
