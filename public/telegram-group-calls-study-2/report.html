Optimal strategy for trading on Telegram group calls

Scope

Accessible history: August 22–October 23, 2026. The archive contains 4,686 messages; 1,532 mention a token, ticker or token link. Of those, 655 came from non-bot user accounts. Mobula identified 486 candidate tokens and 637 human call signals. Another 18 human token-related messages had no unique contract identity, mainly ticker-only or multi-address posts. The execution study uses 408 first sender-token calls with a fresh historical quote, market cap and saved price series. Later repeats by the same sender about the same token are counted but not treated as new entries. With a five-second entry delay, 264 calls have the observations needed for a 60-second sale.

The comparison uses August–September to select a rule and October as a later replay. Calls overlap in time, so the account holds at most one position and never buys the same token twice. Entry and sale need an observed one-second print within ten seconds. A missing sale is a writeoff. Trade size starts at 1% of a $100 account, with a 4% daily full-loss budget. The 10% sale haircut is a stress assumption, not a measured fee.

Rule selection

The grid compares 55 exit rules, 4 entry delays and 60 entry filters. It includes 5–180-second holds, 1.1–3× targets, stops, trailing exits, call-time cap bands, chains, quote age, pre-call trade flow and callers with at least eight earlier calls. Training requires eight scorable calls on eight distinct tokens and ranks the 10%-haircut mean after subtracting one estimated standard error. A rule is selected only if that score is positive. Training selected Caller 01, 1s, stop_0.25_target_3x_120s. The highest training score belonged to Caller 01, 1s delay, stop_0.25_target_3x_120s.

The top training candidate returned +3.85% on the earlier account and -0.15% on the October account at the 10% haircut. At zero haircut those returns were +6.97% and +1.06%. Removing October's two best token trades changes its October return to -0.12% at zero haircut and -1.01% with a 10% haircut. The candidate was chosen from many correlated rules; October is the useful comparison here.

The October replay is sensitive to a small number of trades. Its measured no-haircut gain is small, while the cost stress and the two-winner check both turn it negative. Cash had no simulated drawdown; the selected rule's October drawdown bound was 0.98% at zero haircut and 1.34% with the haircut.
